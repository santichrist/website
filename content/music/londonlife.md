although i generally try to stay away from external analyses of the music i listen to, save for when i've already established some kind of point that i'm confident standing behind and want to actively challenge it, i simply couldn't let go of [this one analysis of *london life* that i saw on youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3ctI6Bxwf3s{:target="_blank"}). while i don't necessarily agree with every point made, i do think that alexandra did a great job at kind of opening my eyes to the extended metaphor that the song embodies, the love for the person the speaker obsesses over, and how it's sort of meant to represent an intense desire. i don't think that fakemink is too known for making revolutionary points in his music, and sometimes i'd even argue that he's lowkey fake deep on his music in an effort to uphold the irresistible model/underground rapper/philosopher shroud that makes him cool, a modern rockstar that everyone wants to be. that being said, i do want to expand on the point of desire made by alexandra's analysis. i want to clarify that i'm mostly just writing my raw thoughts, and this really isn't meant to be any kind of formal analysis or critique to her video, but rather i just want to acknowledge that my thoughts on the song stem from this theme of desire that got brought up.

for what it's worth, i think that the album *london's saviour* as a whole has way deeper themes than people give it credit for. i think that fakemink can sometimes take himself too seriously nowadays with how he's blowing up and grinding away at his career, and while i certainly can't hate on him for trying to speak about broader themes in what is otherwise a rap underground [known for being unserious](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xP_sYT3O-Og{:target="_blank"}), i think that he ironically spoke a lot more deeply about his life's issues in his earlier work before he blew up and deliberately assumed this modern philosopher persona sometime around 2025. not that he can't be deep in his music nowadays, but this album more than anything exemplifies that early hunger that he had, and it's this authentic genius that i think he's currently struggling to integrate into his current music. as put by this otherwise funny ass youtube comment that speaks to how his music is perceived nowadays:

![youtube comment that refers to fakemink as an "algerian shakespeare"](/images/music/londonlife2.jpg#center)

anyway, besides the fact that i needed an excuse to show off that hilarious comment, i've been talking about all this because i wanted to sort of set the context of fakemink's early career, one that to me is governed by that hunger 'to make it' in life and the emotional rawness of a hollow, scary city life that will chew you up and leave you more misguided than before. this album is sort of the culmination of that london misfit edginess, and it's a bit ludicrous to even consider this to be an 'early' work of fakemink's; he's been grinding away at his music career for 8 years before this album was even released in 2023.

# yap yap yap, why does this song stick out from the album anyway?
now that you've had time to appreciate my preamble ramble, i think a good place to start would be with the title _london life_. i was like ok, for an album named _london's saviour_, i guess it would make sense that fakemink is going to shoutout his influence in the london underground scene, so naturally i expected the lyrics of the song to be full of the glamor of the city life. drugs, money, sex, whatever, it's undeniably fun and catchy to fantasize about the high life with lyrics that aren't really meant to mean anything. at the start of the page, i put the chorus of the song, not just because it's catchy and repeated so much throughout the song (those four lines are repeated three to four times ish), but also because it feels so strangely cryptic in the context of the rest of the album. the earlier songs were somewhat more direct in comparison, and often made references to the high-end city life: designer clothes and the paranoia of a drug rush in _kill everything_, the 'made it to the top' bravado and cold weather/jewelry comparison in _just kitten_, and the on-top-of-the-world feeling of the speaker's expensive cocaine rush in _air head_. the pace of each one of those songs also feels a lot faster in comparison, and i guess to me it's analogous to that glamor rush that the speaker gets as he's conquering london. but then _london life_ sort of takes a break and slows down things by a lot more, and the album to me really starts getting a lot more reflective after the speaker comes to acknowledge his obsession with this city glamor in this song.

i think there needs to be an entire discussion on the interweaved instrumental breaks scattered in-between the lyrical songs, being _phantas-ma-goria_, _fleeting feeling_ (the one that precedes _london life_), and _one more, then i'll sleep_, but alas i'll only briefly touch on _fleeting feeling_ since it's the most relevant here. i'm a sucker for this instrumental, and if i'm being honest i don't really feel like i can take away as much from _london life_ if i don't start with this instrumental first, and it was clearly designed to be a slowed down, alternative instrumental of _london life_ that uses the exact same melody in order to transition smoothly. like i said, it's a slowed down version of the instrumental, and i think it's really meant for us to slow down after the rapid, paranoid lyrics in _air head_.

something that i do that's kind of weird is that i like to associate songs and albums with a particular time of the day. i don't think it's based on anything concrete, but rather just the vibe that i get from listening to the piece, or rather the time that i would picture myself enjoying the music the most. if i had to give a couple of examples, i'd say that bladee's _red light_ album is an early morning kind album, not early-riser but rather insomniac-who-stayed-up-too-late. maybe you also haven't heard of this one, but social distortion's song _story of my life_, which is written from the perspective of man looking back at his wasted opportunities in life sort of longingly (what a strange word), is a sunset song, the conclusion of a wasted day bringing to me a similar bout of sadness and reminiscence like in the song. does that make sense? to me, _london's saviour_ is a midnight album, naturally because i tend to associate the glamor of city life with the midnight, where our lovely human vices can run rampantly since we're less inhibited and can just sleep off our sins into the morning.

in that sense, _fleeting feeling_ is like taking a break to stare around in awe at the city, kind of like taking a midnight walk to a convenience store and just admiring all the lights, people outside partying, the bustling traffic of a city that genuinely never takes a break. _london life_, then, turns this state of mental awe into words. source? i've walked around london at night.
