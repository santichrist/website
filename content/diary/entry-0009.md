# fun fact: i'm pretty nearsighted.

i'm currently writing this upstairs in our student center. i remarked last time that i like people-watching, but part of not being able to see anything while i'm up here is that i often have to interpret what i'm experiencing with my other senses. every public space that i go to will have a particular smell (be it old carpet, the hardwood floors, hopefully not the people...) or sound that gives it a certain life that I can experience vicariously. so it's either that or i'll just squint my eyes really hard and mean mug some random, at which point i may or may not feel bad (kidding!)

oh yes, you can tell that this blogpost is going to be particularly neurotic. anyway, the more challenging part is when i actually have to be in the same space, which isn't necessarily a bad thing besides the fact that i...genuinely can't see anything more than like 5 feet in front of me. like i know that i've emphasized a million times at this point that i'm nearsighted (i'm nearsighted), and i find it to be one of those small details that subtly affects my social life in a not so positive way.

sometimes i'll be walking around the quad and someone in the distance will stare at me, dare i say sometimes wave at me, which i may or may not be able to perceive. in this case, it's perfectly excusable to just keep walking on as if i didn't see them, because most people can't really see that far away. if i squint really hard and catch a hint of who it might have been, i might bring it up later. we might even joke about it. the problem comes when i'm walking around a place that is fairly packed with people and i just...can't recognize anyone. it can be very awkward because i'll walk around and swear that i saw absolutely nothing and then go my merry way home, but later on i'll have someone ask me why i ignored them the other day like two weeks later and that happens to particularly upset me because like omgggg i really didn't mean to do that but then i just end up sounding extremely fake. does that make sense?

but that doesn't really explain my aversion to public spaces, does it? what's more than my nearsightedness is the fact that i've grown to develop, because of my nearsightedness, because of my occasional social awkwardness, an aversion to just look at any face in public. i'm not sure exactly when this started, but at some point i realized that i just didn't want to stare at the wrong person accidentally, be it someone i have beef with, be it someone i don't feel like talking to at the moment such that i get stuck in an unskippable cutscene, be it someone i'd love to talk to but i just happen to be in a really bad mood and don't want to drag them down at the moment with my issues. 

like as i entered this student center not 30 minutes ago, i saw someone walking out alone who was then kind enough to hold the door open for me. i definitely had the chance to look at them and stare at them, but i ended up just awkwardly smiling with my head tilted slightly away while avoiding eye contact. it sucks because my friends who are at all socially adept frequently tell me that a good way of getting to meet new people or just lighten someone's day is to just give them a smile. not a cunning, huge cheshire, but just something subtle to let them know that, as a fellow human, their presence ever-so-slightly brightened my day. and in this case this was like the perfect chance to do so, but i just couldn't. i can't.
